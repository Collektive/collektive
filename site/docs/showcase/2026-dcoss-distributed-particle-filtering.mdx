## Abstract

State estimation from uncertain observations collected across a network is central to many cyber-physical and Internet of Things applications.
Distributed Particle Filtering (DPF) supports nonlinear and non-Gaussian estimation,
but existing solutions are often coupled to a particular network architecture or communication model.
This makes them difficult to adapt to open and heterogeneous deployments.

This work proposes a **field-based formulation of Distributed Particle Filtering** grounded in Aggregate Computing.
Estimation and information dissemination are expressed as computational fields,
separating the core particle-filter logic from the mechanisms that coordinate devices and move data through the network.

This separation makes several design dimensions independently configurable,
including the placement and resilience of fusion centres,
the function used to aggregate observations,
and the type and spatial scope of information propagation.
The aim is to provide a common abstraction for engineering different DPF organisations without rewriting the underlying estimator.

## Experiment description

The artefact evaluates the approach through object-tracking simulations in a two-dimensional environment.
A moving node follows a known trajectory,
while a network of observer nodes estimates its position from uncertain local observations.
The repository includes a centralized particle filter as a reference and several distributed configurations built from the same filtering components.

The evaluated configurations cover:

- **Centralized filtering**: one observer performs the complete particle-filter update.
- **Distributed filtering**: estimation state and observations are propagated across the sensor network.
- **Alternative sensing functions**: configurations use continuous, block, and Boolean observation models to vary how local evidence contributes to particle weights.
- **Neighbourhood aggregation**: sensors combine information over a configurable local neighborhood rather than the entire network.
- **Leader-based aggregation**: an elected node acts as the fusion center, allowing the evaluation to include leader replacement after a failure.

Together, these scenarios vary the aggregation function,
the scope of information sharing,
and the placement of the filtering computation while preserving a common Aggregate Computing formulation.

## Results

The experiments show that multiple DPF architectures can be derived from the same field-based framework.
Changing the coordination strategy exposes trade-offs among:

- **accuracy**, as filters incorporate different quantities and forms of evidence;
- **communication cost**, as information is propagated locally or more broadly;
- **robustness**, as fusion is centralized, distributed, or assigned to a replaceable elected leader.

The evaluation positions Aggregate Computing as an abstraction layer for adaptable distributed estimation:
the particle-filter implementation remains reusable,
while coordination policies can be changed to match the topology, sensing model, and resilience requirements of an IoT deployment.
