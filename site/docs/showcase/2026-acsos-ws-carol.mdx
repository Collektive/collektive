## Abstract

Robot swarms need to coordinate their motion while satisfying safety requirements such as obstacle avoidance,
inter-robot separation, and bounded speed.
Centralized controllers can enforce these constraints, but they introduce a single coordination point and do not naturally fit large,
dynamic swarms operating through local communication.

This work presents **CAROL** (Coordinated Aggregate Robotics with Online control Lyapunov and barrier functions),
a fully distributed multi-robot control framework implemented with Collektive.
It connects Aggregate Computing with optimization-based control through **Control Lyapunov Functions (CLFs)**,
which drive robots towards their goals, and **Control Barrier Functions (CBFs)**,
which constrain their motion to preserve safety.

The coupled control problem is solved using the **Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM)**.
Robots perform local and pairwise optimisation steps and negotiate compatible control inputs without a central coordinator.
The resulting safety filter can enforce maximum-speed, obstacle-avoidance, and collision-avoidance constraints,
while treating communication-range preservation as a soft constraint when physical conditions make it impossible to maintain.

## Experiment description

The artefact evaluates the controller through Alchemist simulations covering different coordination patterns and combinations of safety constraints:

- **No obstacle, split targets**: two groups move towards different goals in free space while respecting speed and collision constraints.
- **Common moving target**: the swarm follows a shared moving target, avoids a static obstacle, and preserves connectivity when possible.
- **Leader-follower**: a leader elected through distributed coordination tracks a target while neighbouring robots follow it under obstacle, collision, speed, and communication-range constraints.
- **Different targets**: robots are assigned separate targets and split towards their destinations while avoiding obstacles and one another.
- **Multiple obstacles**: the swarm navigates an environment containing several hazards under the same distributed safety-filtering mechanism.

At every control step, each robot solves a local quadratic program.
The objective remains close to a nominal goal-directed command,
while CLF and CBF terms encode convergence and safety requirements.
The continuous-time dynamics are robustified through zero-order-hold discretisation,
and ADMM terminates when its local primal and dual residuals meet the configured tolerances or the scenario limit is reached.

## Results

The scenarios demonstrate how the same aggregate control architecture supports multiple swarm behaviours without introducing a centralized controller.
Across the experiments, the safety filter combines:

- distributed agreement over coupled control inputs;
- convergence towards common, separate, or leader-defined goals;
- hard limits for speed, obstacle clearance, and inter-robot distance;
- graceful relaxation of communication-range constraints when preserving connectivity would make the local optimisation problem infeasible.

Together, these experiments show how Aggregate Computing can act as the coordination layer for control-theoretic safety mechanisms,
allowing safety constraints and collective behaviors to be composed within the same field-based program.
