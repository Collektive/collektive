## Abstract

Tracking several moving targets from observations collected by a distributed sensor network requires nodes to combine uncertain,
partial information without relying on permanent centralized infrastructure.
Particle filters are well suited to nonlinear and non-Gaussian estimation,
but distributing their computation introduces choices about where measurements are fused,
how far information propagates, and how the sensing network reacts to failures or target motion.

This work expresses distributed particle filtering as a **field-based computation** implemented with Collektive.
Sensors locally execute the filtering logic and use Aggregate Computing coordination mechanisms to select and combine observations.
The formulation supports both neighborhood-based information fusion and dynamically elected fusion leaders,
as well as sensor deployments that move collectively with the targets.

## Experiment description

The evaluation replays three recorded zebra trajectories and deploys a $4 \times 6$ grid of filter nodes.
Each filter uses 250 particles per tracked target,
and each simulation runs for 1,400 simulated seconds to cover the complete trajectories.

Three main scenarios explore different information-flow and deployment strategies:

- **Fixed sensors, neighbor-based filtering**: every sensor remains in place and combines its local observation with those of a bounded number of nearby sensors. The evaluation varies the number of neighbors over 0, 1, 2, 4, and 7.
- **Fixed sensors, leader-based filtering**: observations are routed towards an elected leader, which performs the particle-filter update and exports the estimated trajectories. The active leader is removed after 700 seconds to evaluate recovery through a new election.
- **Moving sensors, neighbor-based filtering**: the sensor grid moves as a swarm around the estimated centroid of the zebras, changing the observation geometry while retaining neighborhood-based fusion. The evaluation varies the number of neighbors over 0, 1, 4, and 7.

All scenarios share the same target trajectories and particle-filter implementation,
so the evaluation isolates the effect of the coordination and deployment strategy.

## Results

The experiments provide a common basis for comparing how field-based particle filtering behaves under different network organisations.
In particular, they expose the effects of:

- increasing or reducing the amount of neighboring information available to each filter;
- concentrating the update at an elected leader and recovering after that leader fails;
- moving the sensing infrastructure with the estimated targets instead of relying on a fixed deployment.

The artefact therefore demonstrates that tracking logic, information propagation, leader election,
and collective sensor motion can be expressed as composable aggregate mechanisms while retaining the same underlying particle-filter model.
